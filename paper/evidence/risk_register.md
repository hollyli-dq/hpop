## C. CLAIM RISKS

### CR1 — TaskBench is component-only evidence
**Risk.** A reader takes the external result as evidence that HPOP works on real
data. It is not: TaskBench exercises only the static partial-order component.

**Why a reviewer catches it.** The benchmark's own preregistration says so in
writing (`scientific_label`: "Does NOT evaluate segmentation, repeated skill
instances, recurrent repair, environment execution, or full HPOP library
learning"). If the paper overclaims and the artifact contradicts it, that is
worse than not running the experiment.

**Severity.** High — it is the most likely single source of an overclaim.

**Mitigation.** The opening paragraph of `results_taskbench.tex` states the
limitation before any result; the caption of `table_taskbench-main` repeats it;
the claim-scope table classifies it as \textsc{component}. Do not remove any of
the three under page pressure.

### CR2 — TaskBench linearizations are not trajectories
**Risk.** Describing the ONE/TWO/FOUR orderings as "observed executions" implies
agent behaviour. They are enumerated from gold DAGs.

**Severity.** High — this one is easy to write by accident and trivial to catch.

**Mitigation.** Say "derived linearizations" or "enumerated from the official
graphs" every time. Never "trajectories", never "executions", never "runs".

### CR3 — The intersection baseline beats the Bayesian model on point recovery
**Risk.** Presenting the Bayesian model as the winner. It is behind on closure
$F_1$ at ONE ($-0.175$) and TWO ($-0.112$) and tied at FOUR ($-0.004$, 24/26
graphs exactly tied). A reviewer who reads the table will see it immediately.

**Severity.** High for credibility, low for the actual claim — the honest claim
(calibration and prediction) is well supported and interesting.

**Mitigation.** Lead with the honest framing, as the frozen verdict itself does:
"Bayesian advantage is uncertainty and prediction". The bolding policy in both
TaskBench tables mechanically prevents bolding an unsupported gap.

### CR4 — AUROC differences are not significant
**Risk.** Claiming a discrimination advantage. Every Bayes-versus-intersection
AUROC interval covers zero ($+0.008$, $-0.036$, $-0.010$).

**Severity.** Medium.

**Mitigation.** AUROC is never bolded in either table; the caption states the
intervals; the draft says so in the text.

### CR5 — τ³ trajectories are a third-party public release
**Risk.** Implying the trajectories are an official τ³-bench artifact. They are
a leaderboard submission from one agent configuration
(Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, thinking mode, gpt-4.1 user simulator, four trials).

**Severity.** High — this is a provenance claim, and provenance claims that
turn out to be wrong damage everything else.

**Mitigation.** `tau3_retail_setup.tex` states it in the second paragraph, before
any statistic, and reports the version-alignment verification that makes the
release usable despite its provenance.

### CR6 — The formal corpus uses supplied U* and a fixed ρ₀
**Risk.** Presenting Conditions A and B as prior-draw calibration studies. They
are not: truth is the Stage 6E2 registered configuration in supplied mode, and
the corpus records $\rho^\ast = $ null, so Condition B fixed $\rho$ at
$\rho_0 = 0.5$.

**Severity.** Medium-high. A methods reviewer will ask whether the truth was
drawn from the prior, and the answer is no.

**Mitigation.** Both facts appear in `results_matched_synthetic_ab.tex` and in
the claim-scope table's "what is not established" cell. A prior-draw calibration
experiment is explicitly out of scope and is named as future work.

### CR7 — β and λ_rep may be weakly identified
**Risk.** Reporting recurrent scalar posteriors as recovered quantities.
The evidence points the other way: λ_rep is the worst-mixing coordinate in every
run (bulk ESS 1076 at 500k sweeps, worst $\widehat{R}$ 1.0051 — the binding
scalar in both), its ESS floor had to be lowered from 1000 to 600 before launch
because the original floor was arithmetically unsatisfiable, and the collapsed-U
forensics recorded that "λ_rep/β block means wander comparably in ALL kernels'
chains".

**Severity.** Medium — only if the paper makes scalar-recovery claims.

**Mitigation.** Make none. Report the scalars as nuisance parameters that are
sampled, converged by R-hat, and not claimed to be recovered. If Condition C
reports scalar posteriors, present them with their ESS.

### CR8 — Condition C result pending
**Risk.** Any sentence that implies joint recoverability before the artifact
exists. Including softened forms: "we expect", "preliminary indications",
"the joint sampler appears to".

**Severity.** Critical — this is the claim the paper most wants to make and the
one with no evidence.

**Mitigation.** `\textit{pending}` placeholders in the table; an explicit
pending paragraph in the draft; C7 marked `pending` in the manifest;
`scripts/validate_paper_evidence.py` fails the build if a pending row acquires
a number.

### CR9 — Condition A's whole-path recovery is weak
**Risk.** Reporting "path strongly identifiable" without the 0.207 MAP figure,
which would leave a reader to discover on their own that exact path recovery is
one trace in five and falls to one in twelve at $J = 48$.

**Severity.** Medium.

**Mitigation.** The draft reports it, stratifies it by trace length, and states
which preregistered disjunct carried the verdict.

### CR10 — Condition B's perfect scores invite a "too good" reaction
**Risk.** Closure, incomparable and reduction $F_1$ all exactly 1.000, posterior
mass 1.000, Hamming 0. A reviewer's first instinct is leakage or a frozen chain.

**Severity.** Medium.

**Mitigation.** The frozen artifacts answer both objections directly, and the
draft uses them: the four chains started from four distinct wrong structures
(`start_manifest.json`: `none_equals_truth = true`), each accepted 24–31
structural changes during burn-in, and the target-parity negative control
detected a deliberate state leak at 3.12 nats. Present the result as an
existence proof on a small matched instance ($K = 3$, $m = 5$), not as a
scaling claim.

### CR11 — The collapsed-U kernel has two recorded historical FAILs
**Risk.** Presenting the kernel as validated without the failure history, or —
worse — being read as having moved the goalposts after failing twice.

**Severity.** High. This is the most attackable methodological point in the
paper, and it is defensible only if told completely.

**Mitigation.** Tell it in order and in full: run 1 FAILED one of eighteen gates
($z = +2.86$); an independent-seed replication FAILED the same gate
($z = +5.76$); no threshold was touched and no larger run was launched; a
separate calibration study established that the gate's IID null is miscalibrated
for autocorrelated prefix-pooled draws, with two independent controls passing;
the replacement statistic is chain-balanced and dependence-aware with a cutoff
frozen by that calibration; the historical statistic is still computed and
reported descriptively. Also report that the final sequential run FAILED at
200k, 250k and 300k before passing at 400k and 500k — the sequential rule is
what makes that legitimate, and hiding it would be worse than showing it.

### CR12 — The burn-in verification artifact records `pass = false`
**Risk.** Quoting `report.md`'s "burn-in 100,000 (verified)" while the
underlying `burnin_verification.json` records `pass: false`.

**Severity.** Low-medium, but it is exactly the kind of thing an artifact-reading
reviewer finds.

**Mitigation.** State the actual situation: one throwaway diagnostic chain's
λ_rep block means left the reference band inside the [25k, 50k] diagnostic
window; the registered burn-in was nevertheless 100,000, four times beyond that
window; the diagnostic chains are never pooled into any validation. See
extraction report discrepancy D3.

## D. COMPUTE RISKS

### CP1 — Two jobs are running right now
`scripts/run_matched_condition_c_formal.py` (Condition C formal chains, started
12:04, ~7.3 h elapsed at extraction, 8 worker processes) and
`scripts/tau3_smoke_fit.py` (τ³ smoke fit, started 20:49, 4 workers). Both were
left completely untouched by this task: not launched, not stopped, not resumed,
not inspected. **Any future paper work must maintain that.**

### CP2 — Wall-time uncertainty on Condition C
No completion estimate can be given without inspecting the run, which is
forbidden. For calibration only, from frozen comparables: the collapsed-U final
validation took 2.52 h for 4 chains × 500k sweeps on the reference problem, and
Step 7B2 took 19.1 h for 4 chains × 50k sweeps on the full corpus at 1.37 s per
sweep per chain. Condition C is a full-corpus joint run, so plan on the
multi-hour-to-multi-day end and do not build the submission schedule around an
optimistic guess.

### CP3 — Resource contention already observed
The TaskBench runtime record notes its 10-core machine was "shared with an
unrelated Condition-C run". Both current jobs are on the same machine. Wall
times measured now are not comparable to wall times measured on an idle machine
— say so wherever a runtime is quoted as a cost claim.

### CP4 — No test-set peeking
The τ³ sealed test split (5 tasks / 20 trajectories,
`test_trajectories.jsonl`) has not been opened and must not be until a
development result is frozen and a test protocol is registered. The TaskBench
test split is already consumed by the frozen result and must not be refitted or
re-tuned; any sensitivity study (B1, B2) reports development results or is
declared post-hoc.

### CP5 — Resumable-chain infrastructure is new
Commit 50eee50 added resumable Condition-C chains. The collapsed-U work has a
frozen precedent that resume is bit-identical (`resume_check.json`: draws,
final state, RNG, counters and schedule phase all identical, with only the
non-mathematical cache-version counter restarting), and Step 7B2 survived a real
power-loss resume bit-identically. If Condition C is resumed, cite that
precedent and re-verify rather than assuming it.
